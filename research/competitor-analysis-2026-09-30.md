## 1. 1-800-GOT-JUNK

**Hudson Valley presence:** No dedicated Hudson Valley franchise found in search results. Nearest locations surfaced: Rochester NY (~200 mi), South Burlington VT (~160 mi), Allentown PA (~140 mi), Somerset NJ (~126 mi). A Bronx franchise exists (BBB profile: 1-800-GOT-JUNK, Bronx NY). **No published Hudson Valley price list.**

**Pricing (national figures, varies by franchise):**
- Volume-based: advertised range **~$107 – $1,099** (1-800-GOT-JUNK YouTube promo: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Fzfcq1ydFbI)
- Minimum charge: **1/8 truckload**; single-item pricing (e.g., fridge) available
- Free, no-obligation **on-site** estimate given before work begins — phone/online quotes are explicitly NOT binding
- **In practice (review-reported):** full truckloads commonly land **$800–$1,800**; multiple reviewers report sticker shock well above advertised ranges

**Services:** Full-service junk removal (they do all lifting/loading/cleanup), in-home removal, curbside options, donation/recycling partnerships (Goodwill, Habitat ReStore), no moving services.

**Review weak spots (1–3 star themes):**
1. **Phone/online quote vs. on-site price — the #1 complaint, described repeatedly as "bait-and-switch."** Examples: phone estimate ~$150 for a couch → $400+ on-site (BBB Bronx complaint, Jun 2026); verbal quote $538 → much higher on arrival; website "full truck $600" → crew's "new pricing list" showed $1,600, final bill $1,800 + expected gratuity, no sweep-up (Yelp Seattle, Jan 2026). Industry survey cited by allmovers.org: **11% of customers report quote-to-bill discrepancy.**
2. **Sticker shock / poor small-load value:** $696 for a dresser, grill, boxes and vases; $783 for cardboard boxes + garbage bags; $1,200 for <1 hour of work ("highway robbery"). Reviewers note the small truck loads unevenly to inflate volume-based pricing.
3. **Damage and refusal of accountability:** BBB review (Nov 2025) — ~$5,500 in damage to a restaurant below the customer's apartment; GM denied liability, company stopped responding to calls/texts/emails. allmovers.org verdict (May 2026): legitimately good for large complex jobs, **poor value for small loads**; 3.5/5 overall.
4. **Pricing opacity:** no standard prices published; phone staff can't quote accurately ("office staff doesn't know the pricing schedule").

Sources: https://allmovers.org/reviews/1-800-got-junk/ · https://www.bbb.org/us/ny/bronx/profile/waste-management/1-800-got-junk-0121-87134307/customer-reviews · https://www.consumeraffairs.com/homeowners/dumpster-rentals/got-junk.html?page=2 · https://www.yelp.com/biz/1-800-got-junk-seattle-seattle-4 · https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-junk-removal-companies/reviews/1-800-got-junk.php

## 2. College Hunks Hauling Junk & Moving

**Hudson Valley presence:** Nearest franchise is **Mount Kisco (Westchester)** — at the southern edge of the Hudson Valley; also Long Island (Hauppauge), Hartford CT, Fairfield NJ. **No franchise found in Orange, Dutchess, Ulster, Putnam, Sullivan, or Rockland counties.** No published hourly rates or flat-fee price list (national policy).

**Pricing (national figures from topconsumerreviews.com, Sep 2026):**
- Online "broad estimate" ladder: **1/8 truckload ≈ $99 · half truck ≈ $459 · full truck ≈ $729** (trucks ~10% larger than competitors per the company)
- Final price set by free, no-obligation on-site estimate; no surprise fees claimed
- Moving: **no published hourly rates**; moveBuddha estimates a typical 2–3 bedroom NY move at **$1,078–$2,463**
- 13 price points internally; factors: volume, item size, hazardous materials/electronics, local disposal costs

**Services:** Junk removal + home/office **moving** + donation pickups + labor services — the only national franchise doing both junk and moving (directly comparable to Junk & Move Pros). Same-day service offered.

**Review weak spots (1–3 star themes, per moveBuddha's Sep 2026 review analyses):**
1. **Estimates rising sharply on moving day:** quoted $550 → charged $1,100; estimated 2 hours → took 8 hours, $2,500 over estimate; "$1,000 over the estimate"; "additional $1,100 due to a 'clerical error.'" Recurring across all franchise analyses (82.9–88.3% positive overall, but pricing accuracy is the top negative driver).
2. **Damage to property + weak claims follow-up:** damaged floors, walls, furniture, cracked TVs; "$2,000 of damage... Company doing nothing"; standard payout limited to $0.60/lb; reviewers repeatedly report **no response to damage claims** and hard-to-reach local offices.
3. **Scheduling failures:** no-shows, arrival windows changed day-of ("booked 9 AM... told 12–2 PM arrival"), demands for full payment upfront.
4. **Undisclosed surcharges:** e.g., $80 mattress recycling fee not disclosed on site.

Sources: https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-junk-removal-companies/reviews/college-hunks.php · https://www.movebuddha.com/movers/ny/college-hunks-hauling-junk-moving-3297/ · https://www.movebuddha.com/movers/nj/college-hunks-hauling-junk-moving-2865/ · https://www.movebuddha.com/movers/tx/college-hunks-hauling-junk-moving-3245/ · https://www.movebuddha.com/movers/ks/college-hunks-hauling-junk-moving-3411/

## 3. The Junkluggers (Lower Hudson Valley)

**Hudson Valley presence:** **218 Market St, Clarkstown, NY (Rockland County)** — this IS a Hudson Valley franchise (https://www.junkluggers.com/lower-hudson-valley/), Google rating **4.9/5 (170 reviews)**. Also Port Chester. This is the franchise competitor physically closest to Junk & Move Pros' market.

**Pricing (from topconsumerreviews.com, Sep 2026 — national sample, varies by franchise):**
- Online sample pricing: **minimum truckload ≈ $159** (~size of a washer) · **half truck ≈ $509** · **full truck ≈ $759**
- **Curbside pickup saves 10%**; possible same-day/next-day discounts (check the site's coupons tab)
- Phone/online quote **may differ** from on-site estimate (the guaranteed price is the on-site one) — reviewers have caught franchises doubling the web quote in person ($850 web → $1,100 on-site)
- **No Hudson Valley-specific price list published**

**Services:** Junk removal, donation pickup (national charity partnerships), curbside zero-touch pickup, **dumpster rental**, labor services; eco-friendly/sustainable disposal branding.

**Review weak spots (1–3 star themes):**
1. **High bills for small jobs:** Thumbtack review (Dec 2025) — charged **$800+ for a less-than-hour job** (half a pickup truck of mixed trash); reviewer: "Won't ever be using these guys again. India wrote to them. They never responded."
2. **On-site price above web quote:** website quoted $850/truckload → charged $1,100 in person; another reviewer's quote doubled on site (topconsumerreviews).
3. **Sloppy operations / poor complaint handling:** left a customer's front door wide open with cats inside; **no response** to written complaints — a recurring theme (also noted with GOT-JUNK and College Hunks franchises).
4. **Thin verified review volume:** topconsumerreviews.com rated the company 2.0/5 ("subpar") in Sep 2026, citing too few verified reviews to trust the brand as a whole; individual franchises range from 5-star to "mediocre and deceptive."

Sources: https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-junk-removal-companies/reviews/junk-luggers · https://www.junkluggers.com/lower-hudson-valley/ · https://www.junkluggers.com/lower-hudson-valley/reviews/ · https://www.thumbtack.com/ny/new-york/the-junkluggers-of-new-york-city/service/ (Thumbtack reviews section)

## 6. Gaps Anthony can exploit (5 concrete plays)

1. **The #1 franchise complaint is quote-to-bill inflation — guarantee against it.** Across GOT-JUNK, College Hunks, and Junkluggers, the dominant 1–3 star theme is the same: the phone/web estimate jumped 50–150% on site ("bait-and-switch"). Anthony's planned on-site calculator should be built around the anti-promise: **publish simple volume ranges (like Hercules publishes hourly rates) and honor the quote on arrival.** This alone differentiates from every franchise in the market.

2. **Undercut franchise pricing 15–25% without racing locals to the bottom.** Franchise full-truck reality is $700–$1,800; Blue Collar's local full truck is $400–$600. A published range between the franchises and the cheapest locals (e.g., targeting **~$125–$500 across load sizes**) makes the "owner-operator, no franchise markup" story concrete and believable. LoadUp's own claim ("20–30% below the competition") proves the undercut message works in this market.

3. **Answer the phone yourself — franchises don't.** Complaints repeatedly cite unanswered calls, ignored emails, and claim black holes after the sale (College Hunks claim non-response; Junkluggers never replying to written complaints; GOT-JUNK GM hanging up mid-call). Publishing **(845) 402-0555** with "you talk to the owner, not a call center" is a real, review-evidenced differentiator — and it supports Anthony's form-to-call flow: submit the form → your info is saved → the owner calls YOU.

4. **One vendor for junk + moving — almost nobody local does both.** College Hunks is the only franchise offering junk removal AND moving; Hercules is moving-only; every local hauler is junk-only. For realtors, property managers, landlords, and estate cleanouts (the exact audience of Anthony's 879-agent campaign), "one call handles the cleanout AND the move" is a unique local pitch.

5. **True same-day speed + damage accountability.** Locals get hit for "crazy delay in pickup" (Yellow Pages review) and franchises run 2-hour arrival windows with no-shows. An owner-operator promising **genuine same-day pickup in Orange/Dutchess/Ulster** can win on speed. Pair it with the accountability angle: franchise damage claims die in corporate/claims limbo ($5,500 restaurant damage denied; $0.60/lb payouts) — "if we damage it, you deal with the owner directly" is a trust story a one-truck operator can tell honestly. **Caution:** do not publish "licensed & insured" claims until verified (per standing review).

## 8. UX recommendation for Anthony's form → auto-call flow

Based on the findings, the highest-converting design is: **(a)** visitor picks service → sees a published price RANGE (not a fixed quote — this is the trust weapon); **(b)** submits name/phone/pickup & dropoff locations → info saved to Supabase `quote_requests`; **(c)** the confirmation screen shows a big **"Call (845) 402-0555 now"** tap-to-call button with a note like *"Your info is saved — call now and the owner will lock in your exact price"*; optionally **(d)** an automated SMS auto-reply ("Got your request — I'll call you within X minutes") plus an instant notification to Anthony's phone. A true auto-dial to the visitor requires a telephony service (Retell/Twilio) and the visitor's opt-in — keep the first version to tap-to-call + instant SMS/owner alert; it's free-tier compatible and matches how Anthony actually works.
