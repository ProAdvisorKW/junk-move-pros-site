## 2. Keywords and topics that rank

**High-intent "how much" keywords** (searchers are buyers):
- `how much does junk removal cost` — 1-800-GOT-JUNK's own video answers "$107–$1,099, volume-based". National guides (wowmover 2026) give: GOT-JUNK **$89–$1,148+**, Junkluggers **$150–$825+**, College Hunks **$399–$999+**, Junk King **$200–$385+** *(ranges from published guides, treat as estimates)*.
- `how much does 1-800-got-junk cost` — movebuddha: **average $240**; full truck ~$600, half ~$400, fridge ~$115, sofa ~$170. Tyler's Hauling (a local competitor!) owns the "How Much Does 1-800-GOT-JUNK Cost and How Our Prices Compare" angle as a blog post — **nobody owns it as a local video**.
- `estate cleanout cost` / `how much does a full house cleanout cost` — Jiffy Junk owns this nationally with embedded videos: whole-house **$1,500–$10,000**; HomeAdvisor average **~$1,250**; hoarding jobs **$5,000–$25,000** *(published ranges)*.
- `how much do movers cost` / `moving company hourly rates` — evergreen, weak video competition from actual movers.

**"Versus / scam / warning" keywords** (the niche's #1 complaint = content fuel):
- ConsumerAffairs/Angi reviews document the exact pattern: $696 for a dresser + grill + boxes; $783 for boxes and garbage bags; $1,236 for a "full truckload" on a truck that arrived 20–25% full. Searchers type things like `1-800-got-junk overcharged`, `junk removal scam`, `why is junk removal so expensive`.
- Proven format: **"[Franchise] quoted $X — here's what the job actually costs"**.

**Local keywords** (low competition, high buyer intent):
- `junk removal [city]` / `estate cleanout [city]` / `movers [city]` for Kingston, Poughkeepsie, Newburgh, Middletown, Ellenville, Monticello, Port Jervis, Hudson Valley.
- Jiffy Junk's playbook is the proof: **state/city-specific pricing pages, each with an embedded YouTube video** ("How Much Does Junk Removal Cost in Illinois/Colorado"). Do the Hudson Valley version on video.

## 3. Content gaps a small Hudson Valley company can own

1. **No active Hudson Valley junk-removal channel exists.** Search turned up only Saint's Junk Removal and Hauling (dormant — last activity ~4+ years ago in the index) and Dumpster Daddy (Capital Region, not Hudson Valley). The entire "junk removal Hudson Valley / Kingston / Poughkeepsie / Newburgh" video space is unclaimed.
2. **Price transparency vs. franchises, on camera.** Nobody has filmed the "phone quote $X → we charge $Y, here's the itemized breakdown" video. Given the niche's #1 complaint is quotes jumping 50–150% on arrival, a local operator showing real invoices is an instant trust wedge — and it doubles as the anti-franchise ad.
3. **Estate cleanout pricing, local edition.** Jiffy Junk owns it nationally; no Hudson Valley operator has a "what a full house cleanout costs here" video. (Internal note: Anthony's $3,500 full-home-cleanout minimum is unpublished — video can discuss ranges without publishing his floor.)
4. **Realtor/landlord cleanout content.** The email campaign is already pitching 818 agents on pre-listing cleanouts — YouTube videos of realtor jobs ("We got this Kingston listing photo-ready in 48 hours") close the loop and give the VAs/agents something to share.
5. **Moving cost transparency.** "How much does it cost to move a 3-bedroom house in the Hudson Valley" — movers rarely publish this; Anthony's 20%-under-competitor positioning ($160/hr vs Hercules $200/hr) is a video waiting to happen.
6. **Donation/recycling proof.** "Where your junk actually goes" — franchises get accused of trashing donations; filming the donation drop is cheap trust content.
