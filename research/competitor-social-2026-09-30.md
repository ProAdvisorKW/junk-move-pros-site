## 1. National franchises

### 1-800-GOT-JUNK?
- **Observed presence:** Official Facebook page (facebook.com/1800GOTJUNK) posts branded carousels pulling **2.6K–3.8K likes** (Feb 2025 "We make junk disappear" carousel: 2.6K likes/43 comments; Apr 2025 staged apartment removal: 3.8K likes/13 comments; Aug 2025 DIY-creator collab "Building Bridget": 2.8K likes/28 comments). Instagram reels from the brand account mostly land in the **4–220 like** range.
- **What actually went viral:**
  - **Creator partnership series** — TikTok creator @josecleanup's "Day 8 of decluttering my parents' apartment" (1-800-GOT-JUNK? partnership): **22.9K likes, 274 comments** on TikTok; the Day 8 IG reel: **21K likes, 470 comments** (instagram.com/reel/DY7anDQF48P). Hook: emotional family story — dad's truck was a "savings account" for 10 years. Comments overwhelmingly emotional: children of immigrants sharing identical stories, praising the brand's kindness.
  - **"60 Second Junk Removal Challenge"** — employee knocks on doors, homeowner points at whatever they want gone in 60 seconds, crew hauls it free. IG version: 110 likes, comments "uniformly positive and aspirational"; FB versions: 6–19 likes. Low reach but zero-negative sentiment — viewers treat it as an invitation, not an ad.
  - **"Millennial Inheritance" giveaway reel** (Jul 2026): 180 likes/21 comments. Hook: "inheriting a storage unit is a burden, not a treasure." Comments full of people sharing what they inherited — the prompt does the engagement work.
- **Recurring formats:** branded testimonial carousels, seasonal memes ("I missed trash day again!" — 880 likes/180 comments), TV-segment reposts, radio-station giveaway collabs, DIY-creator partnerships.
- **Cadence:** *(estimate)* several posts/week across FB/IG; official TikTok handle stats not verified in this pass — brand shows up on TikTok mainly via creator partnerships.
- **What comments reveal:** Franchise comment sections are **dominated by price complaints** — $600 for mattress removal, "$733 vs $180 quote," $1,800 wire-removal allegation, pressure to leave Instagram reviews. The brand replies with a uniform DM-us template. This is the single biggest authenticity opening: the audience is primed to distrust franchise pricing, and says so unprompted under every post.

### College Hunks Hauling Junk & Moving
- **Observed presence:** Official TikTok confirmed as **@collegehunksofficial** (verified via Sep 2026 news coverage of CEO Omar Soliman's apology post). Dozens of franchise/local accounts (Tampa, Dallas Central, Sarasota, Belfair, BucksMont) post near-identical direct-to-camera explainers.
- **What went viral (the wrong way):** A Pittsburgh customer's TikTok accusing movers of mistreating her 79-year-old disabled mother drew **hundreds of thousands of views** and forced a CEO apology video (attackofthefanboy.com, backyarddrunkard.com, Sep 2026). **Lesson: in this niche, customers' complaint videos out-viral corporate content 100:1.** Keep crews camera-trained and the pricing honest, because the camera is always on.
- **Best organic performer found:** Creator Christian Bonnier's "day riding with College Hunks Tampa" ride-along: **260 likes, 23 comments** (instagram.com/reel/DcJ_W4TSUFZ). Hook: "hiring movers is the best use of money" + 7:09 AM team huddle culture. Positive comments laugh at the creator in full uniform; negative comments reuse the thread for damage complaints.
- **Typical franchise posts:** 1–96 likes. The "SAME DAY REMOVAL" word-salad text-overlay explainer is posted by many branches and gets **1–12 likes** — a dead format. The "IF YOUR MOVERS JUST CANCELLED ON YOU" rescue-angle reel: 56 likes.
- **What comments reveal:** Every promotional post attracts both praise ("hardworking young men") and detailed grievance stories (damaged Pottery Barn couch, $100 comp offers, biohazard incidents). The brand's own "transparent pricing" messaging is directly contradicted by commenters — **a local competitor can say the same words and be believed, because the franchise comment sections prove they aren't.**

### Junk King

## 3. What the comment sections say audiences respond to
1. **Price honesty** — every franchise thread fills with quote-vs-bill complaints unprompted. "Own your misquote" content gets shares.
2. **Emotional family stories** — decluttering a parent's home; children of immigrants; aging parents. Hundreds of tearful comments.
3. **Compassion for the vulnerable** — helping elderly/disabled homeowners triggers the longest supportive threads.
4. **Money transparency** — "what this job actually cost / what we made" drives saves and debates.
5. **Schadenfreude at franchise fails** — overloaded trucks, complaint videos. (Use carefully; never fake these.)
6. **Participation prompts** — "what did YOU inherit?", "garage or basement — which is worse?" Comments answer questions, not ads.

## 4. The 10 most copyable viral formats (faceless crew)

All 10 work with **no owner on camera**: hands, POV chest-mount, truck cams, text overlays, AI voiceover.

**1. The 60-Second Point-It-Out Challenge** *(franchise format — 1-800-GOT-JUNK?; local can beat it on authenticity: real neighbors, real free haul, no corporate sheen)*
- Hook formula: "We're in [TOWN] — whatever you can point to in 60 seconds, we haul FREE."
- Example: "60-Second Cleanout Challenge in Ellenville, NY — she pointed at HOW much?!"

**2. After-First Transformation** *(universal; franchises post polished versions — raw phone footage wins)*
- Hook formula: open on the clean AFTER for 2 seconds, smash-cut to the BEFORE.
- Example: "This Kingston garage 6 hours ago vs now 😳 #HudsonValley"

**3. The Honesty Pledge** *(318.junk.removal: 18K plays/1,033 likes — the niche's trust wedge)*
- Hook formula: "If we misquote your job, we eat the difference. Here's why."
- Example: "We quoted $X. It took longer. We charged $X anyway. That's the policy."

**4. Compassion Cleanout** *(Junk Wranglers: 3.4K likes/584 comments — deepest engagement in dataset)*
- Hook formula: "She couldn't clear her porch after surgery. So we showed up."
- Example: "Helping a Newburgh widow get her home back — no charge."

**5. The One-Line Curiosity Caption** *(powerhaulers: 7K likes on "Eviction clean out 😳")*
- Hook formula: mundane footage + one provocative line. Let comments ask the questions.
- Example: "Landlord called. Tenant left THIS. 😳" / "You won't believe what was in this storage unit."

**6. Money Transparency** *(Koerner Office 3.3K likes; Junk Teens 400K followers)*
- Hook formula: "What a full truckload actually costs us vs what we charge."
- Example: "We made $___ on this cleanout — full breakdown." (Ranges only; never the internal floor or markup tiers.)

**7. Franchise Comparison, Local Edition** *(Tyler's Hauling owns it as a blog; nobody owns the HV video)*
- Hook formula: "[Franchise] quoted them $X over the phone. Here's what the job actually took."
- Example: "They called the 1-800 number first. Then they called us. Watch the difference." (Factual, no trademark abuse.)

**8. Day-in-the-Life POV** *(College Hunks Tampa ride-along: 260 likes/23 comments; Junk Removal Solutions HV does it at 3–11 likes — the format is right, hooks are missing)*
- Hook formula: chest-mount, timestamps, real jobs, one funny/real moment per video.
- Example: "POV: 3 cleanouts, 1 dump run, 47 miles — a Tuesday in the Hudson Valley."

**9. Satisfying Load ASMR** *(the niche's background radiation — tossing, crushing, sweeping, truck door slam)*
- Hook formula: no talking, natural sound, fast cuts. Text overlay carries the story.
- Example: "Oddly satisfying: 2 tons of garage junk in 40 seconds 🔊"

**10. Where-It-Went Proof** *(donation drop receipts; franchises get accused of trashing donations — film the drop)*
- Hook formula: "Your couch didn't go to the dump. Watch where it went."
- Example: "This estate cleanout furnished 3 families — here's the donation receipt."

**Formats franchise accounts use that a local crew beats on authenticity:**
- Creator partnerships (1-800-GOT-JUNK? pays creators for decluttering series — a local crew's *real* customer stories need no sponsorship disclosure and read as genuine).
- The 60-second challenge (corporate version feels staged; a real crew knocking on real doors in Ellenville is the same format with 10x the believability).
- Price-transparency talk (franchises *say* "transparent pricing" while their own comment sections call them liars — a local saying it with an itemized whiteboard is believed).
- Compassion content (corporate "community" posts feel like PR; a local crew helping a named neighbor is news in a town group).

**Cautionary viral:** College Hunks' Pittsburgh complaint video (hundreds of thousands of views) and Joey's Junk Removal's billboard controversy (Sep 2026, national coverage) prove outrage out-virals marketing. Train the crew: the camera is always on, quotes never move, and nothing edgy goes up without Anthony's sign-off.
